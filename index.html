E4: Intro to Engineering Design and Manufacturing · Harvey Mudd College · Fall 2025 · Team of 4

Plarch: a squirrel-proof vertical garden

Problem

A campus client wanted a free-standing vertical garden where students could grow and harvest food. His existing garden was being wiped out by squirrels, and the build had to come in on a $150 budget.

What we built

A welded arch of #3 and #4 rebar holding eight planters on three levels, wrapped in galvanized chicken wire with hinged doors and tied into the client's existing drip irrigation. The shelf levels lock in with welded retainer "teeth" instead of being welded on, so the client can remove levels and move planters for sunlight.

Testing and iteration

  • Modeled the frame in SolidWorks, ran a static FEA, and released a 10-sheet drawing package with BOMs, weld callouts, and tolerances
  • FEA couldn't capture how welding changes rebar, so we built a weld-joint test piece and then a full-size prototype
  • The #3 rebar shelf supports deflected badly once loaded. We welded a #4 bar under each one, which brought it down to slight, temporary flex
  • Tested the chicken-wire door with 10 users, who rated ease of use 4.1 out of 5
Load test
80 lb per planter, 2× the design load
Peak FEA stress
2.16 MPa vs. 180 MPa yield
Planting area
15.5 sq ft
Budget
$150.69 of $150, using scavenged planters

E80: Experimental Engineering · Harvey Mudd College · Spring 2026 · Team of 4

Autonomous water-sensing robot

Problem

Measure chlorophyll, pH, and temperature at Dana Point in the ocean and at a freshwater lake, and test whether the two sites differ.

What we built

An underwater vehicle with a PVC frame, foam flotation, and a sealed electronics box, carrying three sensors with op-amp signal conditioning that we designed: a pH probe, a thermistor circuit, and a light-based chlorophyll sensor.

Testing and results

  • Calibrated the thermistor from 0 to 30 °C and the pH sensor at pH 4, 7, and 12
  • Deployed in the ocean and the lake on a safety tether, with thermometer and pH-strip readings as ground truth
  • pH read 7.9 to 8.0 at Dana Point and 6.8 to 7.0 at the lake, matching the ground truth
  • Lessons learned: the thermistor output railed, a bad solder joint cost us the lake temperature data, and ambient sunlight swamped the chlorophyll signal. Next time we'd use a sensor tuned to algal fluorescence

Continuum Mechanics · Harvey Mudd College

FEA stress studies in SolidWorks Simulation

I set up and ran static studies to see how stress actually distributes in loaded parts, then probed results at specific nodes.

  • Cantilever beam with a 1 kN tip load: bending stress (σx) runs from about +1.9 MPa in tension on top to −1.9 MPa in compression on the bottom, peaking at the fixed end
  • Compared shear stress (τxy) and von Mises stress along the same beam
  • Ring loaded on one edge, showing the stress concentration around the hole
