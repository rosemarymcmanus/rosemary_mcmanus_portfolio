Sustainability and Network Design Lab · Harvey Mudd College · Summer 2026

The energy cost of LLM bot traffic

Question

How much does bot traffic, including LLM crawlers, add to a web server's energy use and carbon emissions, and which backend decisions would reduce that footprint?

What I did

  • Took historical Apache logs and categorized more than 700,000 requests by traffic type
  • Replayed the logs on a Raspberry Pi and measured power draw for each traffic type
  • Used the results to study whether a minimal solar-powered server could host the HMC Computer Science Department website
  • Presented the findings on a research poster

Analysis pipeline

Apache logs→Categorize 700k+ requests→Replay on Raspberry Pi→Power and carbon by traffic type

E80: Experimental Engineering · Harvey Mudd College · Spring 2026 · Team of 4

Sensor data from an autonomous water-sensing robot

Question

Do chlorophyll, pH, and temperature differ between the ocean at Dana Point and a freshwater lake?

Collecting the data

We built an underwater vehicle carrying three sensors with op-amp signal conditioning that we designed: a pH probe, a thermistor circuit, and a light-based chlorophyll sensor.

Calibration and validation

  • Calibrated the thermistor from 0 to 30 °C and the pH sensor at pH 4, 7, and 12
  • Took thermometer and pH-strip readings at each site as ground truth
  • pH read 7.9 to 8.0 at Dana Point and 6.8 to 7.0 at the lake, matching the ground truth

Data quality lessons

The thermistor output railed, a bad solder joint cost us the lake temperature data, and ambient sunlight swamped the chlorophyll signal. Next time we'd use a sensor tuned to algal fluorescence.

E4: Intro to Engineering Design and Manufacturing · Harvey Mudd College · Fall 2025

Checking models against reality

For a welded steel vertical garden built for a campus client, I modeled the frame and ran a static FEA, which predicted a peak stress of 2.16 MPa against a 180 MPa yield.

  • FEA couldn't capture how welding changes rebar, so we validated it with a weld-joint test piece and a full-size prototype
  • Load tested at 80 lb per planter, 2× the design load. The shelf supports deflected badly, so we welded a #4 bar under each one, which brought it down to slight, temporary flex
  • Tested the door design with 10 users, who rated ease of use 4.1 out of 5
